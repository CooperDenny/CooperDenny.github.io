Melbourne Pedestrian Traffic Dashboard

An interactive Power BI dashboard over the City of Melbourne’s pedestrian sensor network (hourly counts from about 100 locations since 2010), built to show how foot traffic shifts by hour, weekday and year.

  • Python
  • Jupyter
  • Power BI
  • DAX
Open dashboard full screen Python notebook on GitHub

At a glance

The question

When and where is Melbourne’s CBD busiest, and how has that pattern changed over the years?

What I did

  • Collected the City of Melbourne’s open sensor data with Python and reshaped it into a Power BI-ready table.
  • Built DAX measures for 4-week and 52-week rolling averages to separate trend from weekly noise.
  • Mapped every sensor by latitude and longitude, with slicers for sensor, day of week, weekday/weekend and year.

The dashboard

On a phone? The report is easier to use full screen, rotated to landscape.

Two pages

  • Daily pedestrian count: total daily foot traffic across whichever sensors you select, with rolling averages to show the trend.
  • Pedestrian count summary: average traffic by hour of day, broken down by day of week, weekday vs weekend, and year.

Data and preparation

The data is the City of Melbourne’s Pedestrian Counting System: hourly counts from sensors across the CBD, going back to January 2010.

  • Python handled collection and transformation into a single tidy table for Power BI. The Jupyter notebook is on GitHub.
  • DAX measures add 4-week and 52-week rolling averages, so seasonal swings and long-term shifts are visible at a glance.
  • Geospatial: sensor latitude and longitude drive the map, and a slicer isolates any one of the ~100 sensors.
